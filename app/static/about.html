About this prototype

Catching small fires early, anywhere on Earth

A satellite fire detector that checks any place you pick at 20 m detail, finds fires far smaller than standard fire-monitoring satellites can see, and adds NASA's day and night heat detections from the last 48 hours.

How it works

  1. Photo. For the place you choose, the newest clear Sentinel-2 photo (20 m pixels) of a 10 km square is downloaded straight from the satellite archive.
  2. Look. Three neural networks check every 640 m tile, reading the shortwave-infrared bands where flames glow and the visible bands that rule out look-alikes such as shiny roofs and cloud edges. Their answers are combined.
  3. Day and night. Sentinel-2 only photographs in daylight, every 2–5 days. NASA's VIIRS and MODIS satellites pass about six times a day, day and night, so their detections from the last 48 hours are shown alongside.

"Before" is the earlier version, scored on the same test photos. The gain came from fixing a brightness error in the satellite data and adding fire-free cities and cloud edges to training. Trained on 1,623 Sentinel-2 photos from 6 continents (forest, savanna, peat, volcanoes, oil and gas sites, 100 cities), labelled with NASA VIIRS fire detections and 300 tiles checked by eye.

Limits

  • Sentinel-2 passes every 2–5 days in daylight, so the photo can be days old. Clouds and thick smoke hide the ground.
  • At night only NASA's satellites see fires, and only ones roughly 50 m across or larger.
  • Edges of clouds remain the hardest case for false alarms.
  • It is a prototype for research and demonstration, not an official fire warning service.
